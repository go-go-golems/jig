# Writing a book section

*Kickflips from First Principles* is the workbench's textbook: 69 chapters in a front matter, nine parts (I The board and the bench, II Mathematics of moving things, III Physics, IV Tricks, V The program and its runtime, VI Algorithms of the game, VII Drawing, VIII The API and the tools, IX Gameplay and design) and six appendices (A to F). Each chapter is one Markdown file in `content/book/`, rendered by the Book tab with the same renderer as the notes and exported from there as one HTML page, an EPUB and Markdown. This guide is how to write one chapter, or one numbered section of one, so that it fits the book and passes its checker. The book's own brief is `content/book/BOOK-STYLE.md`; this page condenses it with the checker's rules and a worked example.

## Before you write

1. Read the chapter's entry in `content/book/book.json`: its fixed `title`, its `about` (the brief: cover all of it), its `presets` and `docs`. Read its neighbours' entries so you know what is taught before and after.
2. Read `content/book/FACTS.md`: the program's parameters, systems, states, bodies, the trick table and the running example, all generated by `node build/facts.js`.
3. Read the chapter's presets (`content/presets/<id>.md` and the folder beside it) and shelf docs (`content/docs/`), then the code they point at in `src/lib`, `src/core.js` and `app/`.
4. Write. Touch only the chapter's file.
5. Run `node test/book-check.js <n>` (a number, a letter, or the file name) until it reports no errors, and fix the warnings unless you have a reason.
6. Reread it once as the reader: every term defined before use, every figure matching its caption, every number traceable.

The plan's shape:

```json
{ "title": "Kickflips from First Principles", "subtitle": "…",
  "parts": [{ "id": "VII", "title": "Drawing", "about": "The stage as a technical drawing: …" }],
  "chapters": [{ "n": "47", "part": "VII", "slug": "drawing-motion", "title": "Drawing motion",
    "about": "Onion skins, flight paths, chronophotographs in the manner of Marey, dimension lines.",
    "presets": ["D-03"], "docs": [], "file": "47-drawing-motion.md" }] }
```

## The reader and the voice

The reader reads JavaScript and has high-school mathematics: trigonometry, vectors as arrows, the idea of a derivative. Everything else (dot and cross products, quaternions, inertia tensors, impulses, damping ratios, winding numbers, expression graphs, orthographic projection) is introduced in the chapter the plan assigns it to, before it is used. A chapter that needs an earlier idea uses it and links back (`[Chapter 9](lab:book?ch=9)`) instead of teaching it again.

The model is Peter Norvig's technical writing: foundations first, why before how, concrete before abstract, the reader treated as capable.

- **Start with the point.** The first paragraph says what the chapter explains and what the reader will understand by the end. No preamble.
- **Paragraphs develop ideas.** Bullets only for lists of parallel, complete sentences.
- **Why, then how.** Say what problem a mechanism solves and what breaks without it before showing it. When the design chose, name the alternative and why it lost.
- **Concrete.** Real code, real numbers from a run, real event logs; every abstract claim gets an example within a paragraph or two.
- **No analogies or metaphors.** Explain a system in its own terms. Skating words are the subject, not a metaphor.
- **Define terms once**, in bold where they are defined (**winding number**), then use them the same way everywhere. Use the program's names: thing, component, site, state, param, signal, system, phase, watch, view, plugin, motor, gesture, intent, stance, guide, recognizer.
- **Rider** is the person, **board** the whole skateboard, **deck** the wooden part; `skater` only when naming code (`skater/stance`). Stance is regular unless you say goofy.
- **Frames**: the deck's +x points to the nose, +y out of the grip tape, +z to the toe side. The world's +y is up, gravity −9.81 m/s². One step is 1/120 s, with 8 substeps.
- **British spelling**, SI units: "0.49 s", "81 cm" in prose; $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ with a thin space in maths. En dashes in ranges (1,500–2,500).
- **No em dashes.** Use a colon, a comma, parentheses or a new sentence.

Words and phrases the checker flags: delve, it's worth noting, it is worth noting, of course, clearly, obviously, it goes without saying, in this chapter we will, landscape, tapestry, crucial, seamless, robust, leverage, as you can see, it's important to note, imagine, think of it (this, the), like a, just like, magic, nuanced, paramount, embark, journey, simply, basically, essentially, in essence, here's the thing, let's dive, unlock, and the em dash itself.

## Anatomy of a chapter

```text
# <the chapter title, exactly as in book.json>

Opening: one or two paragraphs. What this chapter explains and why it matters to the game.

## N.1 <first idea>          numbered sections, 4 to 8 of them
## N.2 …
## N.k The running example: <what this chapter shows of the kickflip>
## N.m Key points            complete sentences that summarise; nothing new
## Exercises                 3 to 5, from a two-minute edit to a short project

An optional closing sentence or two connects to the next chapter.
```

- 1,500 to 2,500 words of prose (code, maths and figures do not count; the checker warns under 1,300 and over 3,200). Appendices are as long as their content.
- At least two figures, numbered N.1, N.2… in order.
- At least one quotation of real library code in chapters about the program (Parts I and III to VIII), explained line by line after it.
- A link to each of the chapter's presets (`[D-03 Onion skins and chronophotographs](lab:preset?id=D-03)`) and at least one Try it callout.
- Maths derived, not just stated, with a worked example in the program's numbers.
- Tables for comparisons and for numbers from runs.

## Anatomy of a section

A numbered section (`## 47.2 The memory: the history buffer`) makes one point. It opens with a sentence that states it, gives the reason it matters, shows the mechanism (code, maths or a figure) and explains it, then ties it to a number from the running example or a run. Most sections are two to six paragraphs. A section refers to its figure in the prose ("Figure 47.1 lays them out…") before or right after the figure.

## The running example

One kickflip on flat ground runs through the whole book: preset B-01 at default parameters, played by its key gesture (`controls.routine('kickflip')`: push for 1.6 s, crouch at 2.2 s, flick 0.29 s later, release at 2.52 s). The rider pops at t = 2.525 s, the tail strikes 0.067 s later, the front foot flicks at 2.642 s, the board turns 0.988 times about its long axis, the feet catch it at 2.942 s, the rider lands clean at 3.008 s after 0.49 s in the air, and the recognizer names it a kickflip worth 850 points. FACTS.md has its event log and the deck's state every 0.05 s.

Each chapter that can has a section "N.k The running example: …" showing its part of that kickflip with real numbers: Part II its frames and turns, III its momentum and contacts, IV the trick, V the steps and schedule that compute it, VI the input, motors and recognizer, VII its drawing, VIII how to record and test it, IX its score and feel.

## Figures

A figure is a fenced `svg` block followed by a caption paragraph that starts `**Figure N.k.**`. It is drawn in the stage's style: technical drawing, hairlines, ink, grey and one red, on the reader's background.

- The block starts with `<svg` and has a `viewBox` at most 720 wide (`viewBox="0 0 640 260"` is typical).
- No literal colours: `stroke="currentColor"` or the classes `ink`, `mut`, `acc`, `hair`; fills `none` or `f-ink`, `f-mut`, `f-acc`, `f-fill`, `f-fill2`, `f-paper`, `f-sel`. The Figure component lists what each is for.
- No `<style>`, `<script>`, `<image>`, `<foreignObject>`; well-formed XML (`&lt;`, `&amp;` in text).
- Text 9 to 13 (`font-size="11"`); `lbl` for small uppercase labels, `it` for variables, `b` for bold. Maths in labels as Unicode (ω, θ, ½, ², ₀, ·, −, ×).
- Weights: 1 for outlines, 1.6 for the subject, 0.6 for hatching and construction; `dash` and `dot` classes for dashed and dotted.
- Arrowheads as a marker with an `f-ink` path; ids are made unique per figure, so `arrow` is fine in every figure.
- A figure that claims to show data plots real numbers (from FACTS.md or your own run). A schematic says so in its caption if it could be mistaken for data.

## Code

A `js` block whose first line is a comment naming a file is a **quotation**, and the checker verifies that every other line appears in that file (whitespace ignored). Name files relative to `src/lib` (`// skate/motors.js`, `// rigid.js`), the runtime as `// core.js`, the app as `// app/draft.js`, a preset's own file as `// T-05/main.js`. Leave lines out with a line that is just `// …`. Keep quotations to 5 to 30 lines and explain them in prose afterwards.

Code that is not a quotation (a sketch, an exercise's starting point) has no file comment, must run against the real API, and is labelled as yours in the prose. Never invent an API: check names against `src/core.js`, `content/reference.md` and the library. Pseudocode goes in `text` blocks.

## Links into the workbench

`lab:` links make the book a workbench manual: the reader clicks and the app loads a preset, opens a file at a line, pins a trace, plays a trick. The checker resolves every link against the base library and the chapter's presets. The full list is in *The Markdown the reading views render*. A Try it callout loads the preset first, then acts:

```markdown
> **Try it.** [Load D-03](lab:preset?id=D-03), [switch to front elevation](lab:view?name=front),
> [slow time to a quarter](lab:speed?x=0.25) and [play a kickflip](lab:trick?name=kickflip).
```

Write link text that still reads as prose: in the exported HTML, EPUB and Markdown, workbench links become plain (dotted) text and only links between chapters stay links.

## Maths

KaTeX, rendered as MathML. Inline `$\omega = \dot\theta$` with no space just inside the dollars and no letter right after the closing one; display maths between lines of `$$`. `\begin{aligned}`, `\tfrac`, `\mathbf`, `\hat`, `\dot`, `\lVert`, `\operatorname` all work. Inside a table write `|` as `\vert` and norms as `\lVert … \rVert`. A dollar sign that is not maths is `\$`.

## Facts

Every number about the program comes from the program: FACTS.md, or a measurement you describe ("measured headless with `test/harness.js`, the kickflip gesture in B-01 at default parameters"). Real-world numbers (a deck is about 80 cm long; a street ollie rises 30 to 60 cm) can come from general knowledge, stated loosely. Cite only the classics you are sure of (Newell, Newell and Sancha 1972; Baraff's SIGGRAPH notes; Featherstone; Marey). If the code disagrees with a doc or a note, the code wins: write what the code does and report the difference outside the chapter.

## What the checker enforces

`node test/book-check.js` renders each chapter with the app's renderer.

**Errors** (the chapter fails): line 1 is not `# Title`; an unclosed code fence; maths KaTeX cannot parse; an SVG that is not well-formed, does not start with `<svg`, has no `viewBox`, uses a literal colour or a forbidden element; a `lab:` link that does not resolve; a quoted file that does not exist, or a quoted line that is not in it; the renderer throwing.

**Warnings**: a title different from the plan's; sections not numbered N.1, N.2… in order, or fewer than three; under 1,300 or over 3,200 words of prose; a viewBox wider than 760; a font size outside 8 to 18; a figure without a caption or with the wrong number; fewer than two figures; a preset of the chapter never linked; stray `$` in the rendered text; no exercises; any flagged phrase or an em dash.

## Checklist

- [ ] The title is exactly the plan's; the opening paragraph states what the chapter explains.
- [ ] Sections are numbered N.1 to N.m, four to eight of them, ending with Key points and Exercises.
- [ ] Every term is bold where defined and defined before it is used; earlier chapters are linked, not re-taught.
- [ ] There is a running-example section with real numbers from FACTS.md or a described measurement.
- [ ] At least two figures, captioned **Figure N.k.**, referred to in the prose, classes only, real data where they show data.
- [ ] At least one quotation of real code, short, named, and explained line by line.
- [ ] Every preset of the chapter is linked; at least one Try it starts by loading its preset.
- [ ] Three to five exercises, from a quick edit to a short project.
- [ ] No em dashes, no flagged words, no analogies; British spelling; units with a space.
- [ ] `node test/book-check.js <n>` reports no errors.
