Overview

The stage is a drafting sheet. Bodies are ink hairlines on white paper, seen in orthographic projection, with hatched sun shadows and small monospaced labels; colour appears only in overlays and signals, never in the drawing itself.

This system covers the Form Skate stage: the 2.5D view, its drawing conventions, the code that draws it, and the few HTML parts laid over it. The workbench around the stage (panels, editor, menus, the book) is Form Skate Workbench (https://claude.ai/artifact/5aW5wCqpBBTFm6rtezFLaG); the coding agent is Form Skate Agent (https://claude.ai/artifact/4KJ4g867ubqBbVszAeqXWM).

What is here

  • Tokens. The sheet's colours are the st-* tokens, in a light and a dark theme. The drawing's numbers (line widths, hatch pitch, opacities, dashes, dot sizes, the four views, the sheet furniture) are token families of their own: stroke, hatch, opacity, dash, marks, camera, sheet. Six workbench colours (bg, ink, ink-2, fill, hair, accent) and on-accent are repeated here, with the same names and values, for the hint line, the overlay menu and the fail banner.
  • window.FormSkateStage (components/bundle.js): the stage's own drafting code, unchanged, plus sheet(), which builds a camera and draws a frame the way the stage does. components/index.d.ts lists every call. Use it; do not redraw the look by hand.
  • components/bundle.css: the chips, trick toast, fail banner, hint line and overlay menu.
  • Sections: projection and views, drawing bodies, shadows and ground, depth order, labels and dimensions, overlays, motion, and book figures.

The rules

  1. Orthographic only. Every view is a parallel projection: x = W/2 + s (p − T)·r, y = Y0 − s (p − T)·u. There are four named views: axo (yaw view-axo-yaw 38°, pitch view-axo-pitch 27°), front, side and plan. Orbiting adds to a view's yaw and pitch; it never adds perspective. Lengths parallel to the sheet share one scale, so the scale bar is valid anywhere on it.
  2. Lines, paper, hatch. A solid is an outline stroked at stroke-silhouette (2 px) in st-ink and then filled with paper (st-bg), so it hides what is behind it. On its surface only thin lines: rings and meridians at stroke-ring (0.55 px), opacity opacity-ring. Tone is hatching, never grey fill: sun shadows, the ground band of an elevation, the lot's edge and the faces of props are 45° lines in st-shadow at stroke-hatch (0.6 px), hatch-tile (6 px) apart.
  3. Ink draws; hues annotate. The rider, the board and the props are st-ink (a prop may take one overlay hue as its tone, as D-02's amber cones do). Each overlay has one hue and keeps it: st-violet the rig, st-amber forces, st-rec contacts, the centre of mass and anything that went wrong, st-green sites, st-blue paths, the course and landed dimensions, st-teal the nose path, st-ghost onion skins. Pick a hue by meaning from this list. Do not add hues.
  4. Labels. Canvas text is IBM Plex Mono (canvas-label, 9.5 px, weight 500). Labels beside marks are 8 px in the mark's hue (canvas-label-sm). Dimension values are 8.5 px on a paper patch (canvas-dim). Trick names on the floor are in capitals; numbers carry units and two decimals (1.62 m · 0.42 s); parts of a label are joined by ·.
  5. Depth. Solids are painted back to front. The order comes from a strategy in depth-order.js (deck plane by default) with chain outlines on, so each limb is one outline. A new solid gives itself a name; a flat part that others stand on gives its plane; parts under it say under; parts of one limb share a chain. Use bias (metres) only for a judgment no geometry can make, such as keeping the face in front of the neck.
  6. Layers. Marks are flushed in a fixed order: floor (grid, boundary, floor marks), shadows, ghost (outlines only, never sorted), figure (solids, depth-sorted), overlay (lines, arrows, dots, labels). Put a mark on the lowest layer it belongs to.
  7. Motion is sampled time. The past is drawn from the history (one sample every 1/60 s for 8 s), as ghosts every fifth sample, paths with a tick every 50 ms, or stick figures every 0.08 s, older ones fainter. The trail covers the last jump: take-off to landing plus 0.1 s.
  8. Square and quiet DOM. Chips, the toast and the banner are square (radius-none), 1 px borders, inset stage-inset (12 px) from the stage's corners. The toast lives 2.6 s. Nothing else floats over the sheet.
  9. Dark theme. Every colour is read from the st-* tokens at draw time (readColors), and the stage re-reads them 30 ms after the theme changes. Never hard-code a colour in a view: name a tone (tone: 'blue') and the theme decides.
  10. Sheet furniture. Every sheet has a scale bar bottom left. A sheet at least 640 × 460 px also has a title block bottom right: FORM SKATE, the sheet number, and one row per fact.

Drawing a new body or overlay

  1. Write a view in a library file: $.view('cones', v => v.layer('figure').draw((g, ctx) => { … })). ctx has world read param time frameDt local history events.
  2. Describe the body as stations, ellipses in space { c, A, B, a, b, ring } (centre, two unit axes, two radii, ring flag), from its base up. Put ring: 1 where the girth should read.
  3. Draw it with g.loft(stations, { name, tone, meridian, bias, chain, plane, under, ghost, after }); a flat face is g.poly(points, { fill: 'stage' | 'hatch' | 'soft' | 'none', name }). Shadows, paper fill and depth order follow by themselves.
  4. Annotate on the overlay layer behind a switch: if (!g.on('forces')) return; then g.arrow(p, vector, { tone: 'amber' }), g.dot(p, 2, { tone }), g.label('667 N', p, { tone, dx: 6, size: 8 }). Scale forces in metres per newton and say the scale in the label or the caption.
  5. Press Run in the workbench and look at all four views and both themes. Turn on Order ▸ Truth to see any cells the depth order gets wrong.

Outside the workbench, the same marks go through FormSkateStage.sheet(canvas, { view: 'axo', scale: 150 }).draw(g => { … }, { scaleBar: true }): it sizes the canvas, reads the tokens, builds the stage's camera and flushes the layers.

Content

  • Trick names: lower case in data (kickflip), capitals on the sheet (KICKFLIP · 1.62 m · 0.42 s) and in the toast. A bail adds · bail (HEELFLIP · BAIL) and turns st-rec.
  • Chips are terse capitals (AUTOPILOT, YOU, ● REC 12 s, REPLAY). Ghost chips are hints in lower case (click the stage, then ↑ to push).
  • The hint line opens with a bold state (Click the stage, Riding, Replay) followed by keys and gestures joined by ·.
  • Measurements show two decimals with their unit: flip 0.99 · shove 0.00 · air 0.42 s · +850.

Iconography

The stage uses no icon set and no logo. Glyphs are text: ↑ ← → for keys, ● for recording, ▾ for menus, · as a separator. The title block's mark is the words FORM SKATE in bold Arial.

book-figures.md

Book figures in the stage's look

The book's figures are drawn "in the style of the stage": technical drawing, hairlines, ink, grey and one red accent, on the reader's theme. They are inline SVG, so they cannot use the canvas code; they reproduce its conventions by hand from computed coordinates. The rest of the book's rules (structure, captions, checking) are in Form Skate Workbench (https://claude.ai/artifact/5aW5wCqpBBTFm6rtezFLaG), under writing a book section.

The block

A figure is a fenced block with the language svg, then a caption paragraph that starts **Figure N.k.**. The SVG has a viewBox at most 720 wide, no <style>, <script>, <image> or <foreignObject>, and no literal colours. The page renders it with the workbench's colours, not the stage's: the classes below map to ink, ink-2, accent, hair, fill, fill-2, bg and sel.

Text classes: lbl (small capitals, tracked), it (italic, for variables), b (bold). Line classes: dash, dot. Text sizes 9 to 13.

There are no blue, green, violet, amber or teal classes. A figure that shows an overlay draws it in ink or mut and puts the one thing that matters in acc; the caption names what each line is.

Compute, do not guess

Use the stage's own camera for the coordinates. In a page that loads this system's bundle:

const S = window.FormSkateStage, v = S.VIEWS.axo;
const cam = S.makeCam(v.yaw, v.pitch, 120, [0, 0, 0], 640, 260);   // 120 px/m, centred on the origin
const p = S.proj(cam, [1, 0, 0]);                                    // a world point on the 640 × 260 sheet
const g = new S.Draft(); g.cam = cam;
const { E } = g.loftPath(S.board.sectionsOfDeck(S.board.DECK));      // each section's 20 projected points
const outline = S.hull2(E[8].concat(E[9]));                          // one hull between neighbouring sections

Write the numbers into the SVG rounded to 0.1 px. A figure that claims to show data (a path, a pose, a force) plots real numbers from the book's FACTS.md or a recorded run; a schematic looks like one and says so in its caption.

Figure checklist

  • One subject in ink at 1.6, the rest at 1 or 0.6.
  • One accent, used for the point of the figure.
  • Real scale stated in the caption (at 120 px/m) when lengths can be read off the figure.
  • Every label names a thing the prose names, in the same words, with units.
  • The caption says which view (axonometric, front, side, plan) and which moment (t = 2.783 s).

depth-order.md

Depth order

The stage hides surfaces with the painter's algorithm: solids are drawn back to front, each stroked and then filled with paper, so whatever comes later hides what came before. Choosing the order is the whole of hidden-surface removal in a hairline drawing. A wrong order puts a foot under the deck it stands on.

The pipeline

  1. Every solid mark (loft or poly, not on the floor or ghost layers) becomes an item (draft.js:88-99): { uid, name, k, S | pts, o, cen, key }. uid numbers repeated names per frame (wheel#3); key is the depth of the centroid along cam.c plus o.bias. Larger is nearer.
  2. A strategy from src/lib/depth-order.js returns the items back to front. A strategy that throws, or returns anything but a reordering of its items, is ignored for that frame and the stage sorts by key (draft.js:129-136).
  3. With chain outlines on (the default), the parts of each limb are ordered and drawn as one unit (unitOrder, truth.js:115-125): all their outlines, then all their fills, so the seams at knees, elbows and waist vanish. A chain sits at the mean place of its parts and keeps last frame's place unless that moves by more than three places.
// src/lib/depth-order.js:100-109
exports.strategies = {
  'centroid': (items) => byKey(items),
  'coherent': (items, cam, mem) => coherent(items, mem, 0.02),
  'deck plane': (items, cam, mem, kit) => anchored(items, cam, mem, kit, 0),
  'deck plane + coherent': (items, cam, mem, kit) => anchored(items, cam, mem, kit, 0.02),
  'deck plane + coherent + smoothed keys': (items, cam, mem, kit) => anchored(items, cam, mem, kit, 0.02, smoothKeys(items, mem)),
};
exports.current = 'deck plane';

Why one key per part fails

  • A long part with a small one on it. The deck is 81 cm long and the front foot stands near one end. Whenever the nose points slightly away from the viewer, the deck's centroid sorts in front of the foot standing on it; in the tracked side view by 12.1 cm. With one key per part the front foot was drawn under the deck in 51% of frames in the axonometric view and 76% in the tracked side view.
  • Parts that pass through each other. At every joint two lofts overlap in space. No whole-part order is right there.
  • Near-ties flicker. Two keys within a centimetre swap as contact noise moves the parts by millimetres.

The deck as a plane

A flat part orders exactly anything wholly on one side of its plane (Newell, Newell and Sancha, 1972). planeVerdict(f, q, cam) (depth-order.js:42-55) measures a part's extent along the plane's normal from its sections' surfaces, not their centres; counts a part 75% on one side as that side; and leans edge-on cases toward the rider (lean: 0.1). anchored (depth-order.js:61-89) then moves the deck's key just behind the nearest part standing on it and its undercarriage just behind the deck.

So, when you draw:

  • give a flat part that others stand on plane: { n, p0, tol, lean }, with n its up normal and p0 a point on the standing surface (the deck uses tol: d.thick, lean: 0.1; board.js:150);
  • give the parts under it under: '<its name>';
  • give the parts of one limb the same chain;
  • use bias only where geometry cannot decide (the head is biased 6 cm forward so the face is never hidden by the neck).

Measuring an order

The Truth tool (Order menu, or overlays: ['truth'] in sheet()) samples every solid's surface into 3 px cells (truth-cell), keeps the nearest part per cell, and paints in st-rec at opacity-truth (0.5) every contested cell the order draws wrong (truth.js:8-91). It is recomputed a few times a second while playing. Order changes outlines in st-rec at stroke-changes (2.4 px), fading over 18 frames, every overlapping pair whose order just flipped.

On 45 s of the autopilot, the deck plane with chain outlines brought the axonometric view from 468 to 182 cm² drawn wrong per frame, and the foot under the deck from 51% to 1.9% of frames. What is left is mostly at joints.

Writing a strategy

A strategy is (items, cam, mem, kit) → items back to front. cam gives c, r, u; mem persists between frames for this stage; kit.overlaps(a, b) says whether two silhouettes overlap on the sheet (a box test, then hulls of neighbouring sections) and kit.depth(p) gives a depth. Add it to exports.strategies, press Run, pick it in the Order menu, and score it on a clip in Experiments.

drawing-bodies.md

Drawing bodies

Every body on the stage is a loft: a list of elliptical sections (stations) swept along it, drawn in a few hairlines. The rider, the deck, the trucks, the wheels and D-02's cones are all lofts. Flat faces (a funbox, a kicker) are polygons.

Stations

A station is an ellipse in space, c + a cos θ A + b sin θ B:

field meaning
c centre, a world point in metres
A, B unit axes of the section's plane, perpendicular
a, b radii along A and B, metres
ring 1 to draw this section as a visible ring

List stations from one end of the body to the other. Use more of them where the outline bends: the deck has 18, dense at the nose and tail (sectionsOfDeck, board.js:131-137); D-02's cone has a two-station base plate and seven stations up its body.

How a loft is drawn

// app/draft.js:308-322
drawLoft(ctx, S, o, ghost) {
  const cam = this.cam, C = this.C, { path, E } = this.loftPath(S);
  ctx.lineJoin = 'round';
  if (ghost) { /* 0.8 px outline in the ghost tone, faint paper wash: see motion */ return; }
  const tone = toneOf(C, o.tone, C.ink);
  ctx.strokeStyle = tone; ctx.lineWidth = 2; ctx.stroke(path); ctx.fillStyle = o.fill === 'soft' ? C.soft : C.stage; ctx.fill(path);
  ctx.lineWidth = 0.55; ctx.globalAlpha = 0.7;
  S.forEach((s, i) => { if (s.ring) visibleArc(ctx, cam, s, E[i]); });
  if (o.meridian) meridian(ctx, cam, S, [0, 1]);
  ctx.globalAlpha = 1;
}
  1. Silhouette. Each section is projected to 20 points. Each pair of neighbouring sections gives one convex hull on the sheet (Andrew's monotone chain, hull2). The union of the hulls is the outline. Pairs, not the whole stack, so a bent body stays bent.
  2. Stroke, then fill. The union is stroked at stroke-silhouette (2 px) and filled with paper (st-bg, or st-soft with fill: 'soft'). The fill covers the inner half of the stroke and every seam between hulls, leaving one 1 px outline that hides what is behind it.
  3. Rings. A station with ring: 1 draws the half of its ellipse whose outward normal faces the viewer, at stroke-ring (0.55 px) and opacity-ring (0.7). Rings are where girth reads: a waist, a band.
  4. Meridian. With meridian: true, a line joins the end of every station's B axis along the body, drawn only where it faces the viewer: the torso's spine, a cone's seam.

The options

g.loft(stations, o):

option use
name the part's name; numbered per frame for the depth order (wheel#3)
tone a palette name ('amber') or a CSS colour for the outline; default st-ink
fill 'soft' for st-soft instead of paper
meridian draw the meridian
bias metres added to the depth key; a judgment, used sparingly (head +0.06, deck +0.02, wheels −0.015)
plane { n, p0, tol, lean }: a flat part that orders what stands on it (the deck)
under the name of the flat part this one sits under ('deck' for trucks, hangers, wheels)
chain a limb name; with chain outlines on, a chain is stroked and filled as one outline
ghost a number from 0 to 1: draw as an onion-skin outline at that strength
noShadow cast no shadow
after d => …, draws decals once the loft is painted

g.poly(points, { fill, tone, width, stroke, name, bias }) draws a flat polygon, depth-sorted with the lofts. fill: 'stage' (default) is paper, 'hatch' is paper and then the hatch, 'soft' is st-soft, 'none' leaves it open; stroke: false drops the 1 px edge.

The figure

The rider is 19 lofts on a 22-joint skeleton (figure.js:7-15, 75-101). Each station is fixed to a joint: an offset in the joint's frame, the joint's x axis as A and its z axis (or y, for the feet and toes) as B. Pose the skeleton and the stations follow:

// src/lib/skate/figure.js:134-137
for (const part of PARTS) {
  const st = part.st.map(([j, o, a, b, ring]) => { const W = F.W[j]; return { c: toW(F, j, o), A: Q.rot(W, [1, 0, 0]), B: Q.rot(W, part.ax === 'xy' ? [0, 1, 0] : [0, 0, 1]), a, b, ring: ring || 0 }; });
  g.loft(st, { bias: part.bias, meridian: part.meridian, name: part.key, chain: part.chain, after: part.key === 'head' ? d => drawFace(d, F, s) : null });
}

Parts belong to six chains: legL, legR, armL, armR, head and torso (figure.js:78). Upper arms, forearms, thighs and shins end in capped stations (three shrinking sections), so their ends are round. The figure is a view: it reads the simulation and never pushes on it. Its pose is closed-form each frame: posture springs, forward kinematics, two-bone IK onto the feet, forward kinematics again.

The board

drawBoard(g, pose, dims, { ghost, roll }) (board.js:138-168) lofts the deck from its 18 sections (rings where the kicks start and over the trucks), then per truck a baseplate, a hanger and two wheels, and four bolts as 0.9 px dots that hide when the grip faces away. The deck carries its plane so riders stand in front of it; trucks, hangers and wheels are under: 'deck'. With ghost set, only the deck is drawn.

Decals

Features on a surface (the face) are drawn in its tangent plane with one canvas transform, exact under an orthographic camera: in a loft's after, d.decal({ p, q }, local, normal, size, mirror, ctx2d => …) maps a unit square at local in the frame { p, q } onto the sheet, and returns false when the normal faces away. Draw inside it with ordinary 2D calls and d.color('ink'). The face uses 0.17 to 0.42 unit strokes in that square (figure.js:186-199).

labels-and-dimensions.md

Labels and dimensions

Text on the sheet is small, monospaced and placed by the world point it describes. It states a fact with its unit; it never decorates.

Labels

// app/draft.js:357-358 and 374-379
const p = proj(cam, m.p); this.text(ctx, m.text, p[0] + (o.dx || 0), p[1] + (o.dy || 0), { size: o.size || 9.5, col, align: o.align, bg: o.bg ? C.stage : null, w: o.weight });
text(ctx, s, x, y, o = {}) {
  ctx.font = `${o.w || 500} ${o.size || 10}px ${o.mono === false ? 'Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif' : MONO}`;
  ctx.textAlign = o.align || 'left'; ctx.textBaseline = o.base || 'middle';
  if (o.bg) { const w = ctx.measureText(s).width, h = (o.size || 10) + 4, x0 = o.align === 'center' ? x - w / 2 : o.align === 'right' ? x - w : x; ctx.fillStyle = o.bg; ctx.fillRect(x0 - 3, y - h / 2, w + 6, h); }
  ctx.fillStyle = o.col || this.C.ink; ctx.fillText(s, x, y);
}

g.label(text, p, { dx, dy, size, align, bg, weight, tone }):

  • The face is IBM Plex Mono at weight 500, vertically centred on the point. Sizes are CSS pixels and do not change with zoom.
  • 9.5 px (canvas-label) is the default: a free label, a title-block value.
  • 8 px (canvas-label-sm) is a label beside its own mark, in the mark's hue, offset a few pixels: COM at dx: 7, dy: -7; a force 667 N at dx: 6 past the arrow's tip; a site name at dx: 6, dy: -6.
  • 8.5 px (canvas-dim) is a dimension value.
  • bg: true puts a paper patch 3 px wider on each side and 4 px taller than the text behind it, so the label breaks a line instead of crossing it. Use it whenever a label sits on a line or a hatch.
  • weight: 700 is for a heading on the sheet (a compare sheet's name), not for emphasis inside a label.

The only text not in Plex is the title block's FORM SKATE, bold Arial at 9.5 px (canvas-title).

Writing labels

  • Name things as the code and the book name them: COM, frontBolts, centre around the foot.
  • Numbers carry units and fixed decimals: 0.42 s, 1.62 m, 667 N; a count of turns has two decimals (flip 0.99).
  • Join parts with ·: KICKFLIP · 1.62 m · 0.42 s.
  • Trick names on the floor are in capitals; everything else keeps its case.
  • Keep a label to one line. If it needs a sentence, it belongs in the panel beside the stage or in the book.

Dimension lines

g.dimension(a, b, text, { offset, tone, alpha }) measures the horizontal distance between two floor points:

// app/draft.js:362-373
const a = m.a, b = m.b, d = V.sub(b, a), L = Math.hypot(d[0], d[2]); if (L < 0.05) return;
const n = [-d[2] / L * off, 0, d[0] / L * off];
const A = V.add(a, n), B = V.add(b, n), pa = proj(cam, a), pb = proj(cam, b), qa = proj(cam, A), qb = proj(cam, B);
ctx.lineWidth = 0.7; /* extension lines from a and b to 12% past the dimension line */
ctx.lineWidth = 1;   /* the dimension line from A to B */
for (const q of [qa, qb]) { ctx.beginPath(); ctx.moveTo(q[0] - 4, q[1] + 4); ctx.lineTo(q[0] + 4, q[1] - 4); ctx.lineWidth = 1.4; ctx.stroke(); }
this.text(ctx, m.text, mid[0], mid[1], { size: 8.5, col, align: 'center', bg: C.stage });
  • The line is offset sideways by offset metres (default 0.4; the trick dimensions use 0.45), to the left of the direction from a to b.
  • Extension lines are stroke-dim-ext (0.7 px) and overshoot by dim-overshoot (12%); the line is stroke-overlay (1 px); the end ticks are stroke-dim-tick (1.4 px) diagonals dim-tick (8 × 8 px) in screen space, so they keep their size at any zoom.
  • The value sits centred on the line, on a paper patch.
  • It measures horizontal distances only: points closer than 5 cm in plan draw nothing, so a height needs g.line and g.label instead. It is at true length in plan and foreshortened in the other views.

The Dims overlay (annotations) draws one for each of the last four tricks, take-off to landing: st-blue when landed, st-rec for a bail, with opacity 0.35 + 0.65 (i + 1)/n so the newest is solid (recognizer.js:149-158).

Sheet furniture

  • Scale bar (draft.js:407-414), always, bottom left: 14 px from the left, 18 px from the bottom (scale-bar-inset). One metre, or 25 cm if a metre would pass 30% of the width, or 5 m if a metre is under 40 px. Quarters, the first and third filled 2 px high; 0 and the length at 9 px. It is exact for lengths parallel to the sheet; in the axonometric view a metre along x is 0.836 of it and a metre up 0.891.
  • Title block (draft.js:399-406), bottom right, only on a sheet at least 640 × 460 px (title-block-min): a 250 px box (title-block-width), 10 px from the edges, a 17 px header with FORM SKATE and SHEET <id> (the preset's id, R-01 in replay), then 15 px rows (title-block-row) of key (9 px, st-muted) and value (9.5 px; a value over 25 characters is cut to 24 and an ellipsis). The live stage shows PRESET, LAST TRICK, SPEED and VIEW.

motion.md

Motion

A still sheet shows several moments at once by sampling time evenly: equal times apart, so spacing shows speed. The stage keeps the past in a history, draws it under the solids, and fades it with age.

The history and the trail window

The runtime keeps every body's pose { p, q } every second step for 8 s: 480 samples, one per 1/60 s. Views read it with ctx.history.between(t0, t1). The window a view should draw is g.trailSpan():

// app/ui/stage.jsx:32-42
export function trailSpan(host) {
  const rt = host.rt; if (!rt || !host.stage.overlays.has('trail')) return null;
  const h = rt.history.buf; if (!h.length) return null;
  const last = type => rt.events.list({ type, last: 1 })[0];
  const to = last('takeoff'); if (!to || to.t < h[0].t) return null;
  if (rt.ir.states.has('skater/phase') && rt.read('skater/phase') === 'air') return [to.t, rt.time];
  const ends = [last('land'), last('bail')].filter(e => e && e.t >= to.t).map(e => e.t);
  if (!ends.length) return null;
  const t1 = Math.min(...ends) + 0.1;
  return rt.time - t1 < 6 ? [to.t, t1] : null;
}

It is the last jump: from its take-off to now while in the air, then to its landing or bail plus 0.1 s, kept for 6 s. Both ends come from the stance controller's own events, which arrive in the step they happen. Key any new motion view to this window, not to the trick recognizer's event, which arrives 0.233 s after the landing.

Drawing between steps

The world steps every 1/120 s; the screen draws at its own rate. The stage draws a blend of the last two steps, alpha of the way from the previous to the current one, alpha being how far the clock has run into the next step (host.js:201-230). Positions and velocities are interpolated linearly and orientations by slerp; the bodies are put back exactly after the frame, so nothing measured ever sees the blend. A teleport (more than 0.5 m, or |q·q′| < 0.7) is drawn where the body is now. The blend is skipped after a single step, a reset or a hot reload. Without it, a frame that gets one step more or fewer than the last puts the board up to one step of travel (3.3 cm at 4 m/s) off the smoothly gliding camera, which reads as blur at speed.

Rules for a new overlay

  • Gate it: if (!g.on('<name>')) return; at the top of the view, so it costs nothing when off.
  • Draw it on the overlay layer (over the solids) unless it lies on the ground (floor, under them, as the course and the dimensions do) or is a past state (ghost).
  • Take one hue from the table by meaning. Forces are amber, contacts and failures st-rec, kinematics violet, positions and paths blue, names of places green. A view that needs a second series uses st-teal.
  • Draw vectors to a stated scale in metres per unit and write the scale into the label or the caption. Scales differ between overlays (Forces 1 m per 2,500 N, Contacts 1 m per 1,200 N), so arrows from two overlays cannot be compared by eye.
  • An arrow is a projected world vector: it is foreshortened like any line, true length only parallel to the sheet. Look along the vector's plane to read it.
  • Impulses and angular nudges have no lasting force or point; no overlay draws them.

projection-and-views.md

Projection and views

The stage projects the world with two dot products. A view is an orthonormal basis: c points from the scene toward the viewer, r is the sheet's right and is always horizontal, and u = c × r is the sheet's up. World units are metres; +y is up.

The camera

// app/draft.js:16-22
export function makeCam(yaw, pitch, scale, target, W, H) {
  const cp = Math.cos(pitch), c = [cp * Math.sin(yaw), Math.sin(pitch), cp * Math.cos(yaw)];
  const r = [Math.cos(yaw), 0, -Math.sin(yaw)], u = V.cross(c, r);
  return { c, r, u, s: scale, T: target, W, H, yaw, pitch, Y0: H * (pitch > 1.2 ? 0.5 : 0.5) };
}
export const proj = (cam, p) => { const d = V.sub(p, cam.T); return [cam.W / 2 + V.dot(d, cam.r) * cam.s, cam.Y0 - V.dot(d, cam.u) * cam.s]; };
export const depth = (cam, p) => V.dot(V.sub(p, cam.T), cam.c);
  • s is pixels per metre. T is the world point drawn at the sheet's centre, (W/2, Y0); Y0 is half the height in every view.
  • depth(cam, p) grows toward the viewer. It is what the depth order sorts on.
  • unprojectFloor(cam, x, y, h = 0) (draft.js:23-28) turns a sheet point back into the floor point under it. It returns null when the camera looks at the floor edge-on (|c.y| < 0.05).

Because r has no vertical part, a vertical world line is drawn vertical in every view except the plan. Parallel lines stay parallel, an ellipse projects to an ellipse, and a drawing in a body's tangent plane is one affine transform. A perspective camera would break all three; do not add one.

  • Orbit: drag. Yaw changes by orbit-rate (0.008 rad) per pixel across, pitch by 0.006 rad per pixel down, with the pitch offset kept within −1 to 1.6 rad (stage.jsx:139). A drag starts after drag-threshold (4 px).
  • Pan: Shift-drag, or drag with the right or middle button. The pan is an offset of the target along the camera's own r and u, in metres, so the drawing stays put on the screen while the camera follows, tracks or orbits:

Follow and track

The camera follows the board with critically damped springs on wall-clock time (stage.jsx:80-89): the target eases toward the board plus follow-lead (0.4 s) of its velocity with half-life follow-half-life (0.32 s; 0.6 s with Follow off), and with Track on the yaw eases toward the rider's heading with half-life track-half-life (0.5 s). A paused or scrubbed replay snaps the camera instead of easing it.

shadows-and-ground.md

Shadows and ground

Height is the one thing an orthographic sheet hides. The plan projects it away, the axonometric view confuses it with distance, and the elevations show it but lose depth. The stage puts it back with two conventions: a hatched sun shadow when the camera looks down, and a cut ground line when it does not.

The sun

// app/draft.js:138
export const SUN = V.norm([-0.5, -1, -0.38]);

SUN is the direction the light travels (token sun). The sun stands 57.9° high in the +x, +z quarter of the sky, behind the viewer's right shoulder in the default axonometric view, so shadows fall away from the viewer and a little to the left. A point at height h above the ground casts its shadow 0.628 h away.

Drawing shadows

A shadow is a second, oblique projection: each point is pushed along SUN down to the ground (the terrain's height there, heightAt(x, z), or 0), then projected with the camera.

  • Shadows are drawn after the floor and before everything that casts them.
  • Only lofts cast shadows. Polygons (props), ghosts and lofts marked noShadow do not.
  • Shadows appear only when the camera looks down by more than 0.05 rad (2.9°, planar). In the elevations they would collapse onto the ground line.
  • The Shadows overlay switches them; it is on by default.

The tile is hatch-tile (6 px) square: one line corner to corner rising to the right at 45°, overshooting a pixel at each end, plus the two corner strokes that join it to its neighbours. Repeated, it gives stroke-hatch (0.6 px) lines 6 px apart along a row and 4.2 px apart across them, in st-shadow. It is drawn at twice the size and scaled by half, so it stays sharp on dense screens, and cached per canvas and colour. The hatch keeps the drawing to lines: the floor grid shows between the hatch lines, and a dark sheet gets light hatching from the same token.

The floor

  • Grid (g.grid({ minor, major, extent }), draft.js:223-230): lines at stroke-grid (0.6 px), st-grid every minor metres and st-major every major. The flat ground uses 0.5 m and 2 m (terrain.js:8). Only the part the camera sees is drawn. The Grid overlay switches it, and it is drawn only when the camera looks down.
  • Ground line (draft.js:210-215): in an elevation the ground is a section cut. A stroke-ground (1.4 px) st-ink line at the ground's height, over a hatch-ground-band (18 px) band of 0.6 px st-muted lines hatch-ground-pitch (7 px) apart. It replaces shadows in exactly the views that cannot have them.
  • Boundary (g.boundary({ size, band }), draft.js:231-238): the lot's edge, a hatched band boundary-band (0.35 m on the flat ground) between an outer and an inner square, filled with the even-odd rule and outlined at 1 px on both sides.
