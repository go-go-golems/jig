# Spur gear pair

An 18-tooth pinion driving a 36-tooth wheel, 2:1, on a printed base whose two pegs stand exactly 54 mm apart. Turn the pinion twice and the wheel turns once. The idea it teaches: the centre distance of a gear pair is not a dimension you choose. It follows from the module and the tooth counts, a = m (z₁ + z₂) / 2, and here `j.inspect.gearMesh` computes it and the base is built from its answer.

> **Try it.** [Look at it from above](lab:view/stage?view=plan) to see the teeth in mesh. [Turn the wheel back to 0°](lab:param/wheel=0): a wheel tooth now points straight at a pinion tooth, and the interference check fails. [Set it to 5°](lab:param/wheel=5), half a tooth, and the gap is back.

## How it is built

Both gears are `j.lib.gear.spur({ module: mm(2), teeth, width: mm(8) })`: exact involute flanks generated by the ISO 53 rack, a trochoidal root fillet and 1.25 m of dedendum. Each gets a bore for its peg with `j.fit.hole(peg, 'free')`, Ø5.7 mm on a Ø5 mm peg.

`j.inspect.gearMesh(pinion, wheel)` checks that the modules and pressure angles match and reports the centre distance, 54 mm, the contact ratio, 1.61 (more than one tooth pair always in contact), the ratio, 2:1, and the backlash, 0.4 mm. If the pair did not mesh the script stops with the reasons. The base is a slot-shaped bar from axle to axle with a 0.6 mm spacer and a 14 mm peg at each end, all placed at `a`.

## The assembly

The script returns an assembly. The base is grounded; each gear's `axis` port sits on a port of the base by a revolute joint, so the assembly has 2 degrees of freedom (a gear mesh is not a joint here, so the two turn independently in the model). The wheel's joint starts at 180°/36 = 5°: 36 is even, so without that half-tooth turn a wheel tooth would point straight at a pinion tooth.

`.interference()` measures the smallest gap between every two parts: 0.200 mm between pinion and wheel at this pose, half the backlash on each side, and 0.200 mm between each gear and the base, because the axle ports sit 0.2 mm above the spacers.

## Printing it

All three parts flat, gears on a face, so every tooth is a vertical prism: about 28 g of PLA and 75 min. The print job is in the script; set `printJob` to `true` to compile it. It is not the default because the job's slicing pass takes about a minute on involute flanks in this engine, against a few seconds for the assembly.
