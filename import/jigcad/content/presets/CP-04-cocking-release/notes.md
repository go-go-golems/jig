# Cocking and release

Follow the setting lever inward to its toothed ring. The ring turns the pinion, whose lug winds the main drive cam. At the end of the stroke the inner lever catches the cam. The outer lever's projecting foot then lifts the inner lever clear for release.

> **Try it.** Look down on the toothed sector in [plan](lab:view/stage?view=plan), then inspect the height of the pinion lug from the [side](lab:view/stage?view=side).

## Evidence and fit

The factory 0-MXV GH Reflex 103 drawings CN-1210-022, plates 2 and 3 (manual PDF pages 81 and 83), identify 528 and 517. Lesson 9 figures 39–42 show the functional sequence on a 00-MXV. Figure 79 shows the adjustable inner-lever eccentric on the bridge. The CAD uses a fixed pivot here. The return springs, eccentric adjustment, secondary catch on the cocking ring and enforced gear/contact motion are outside this study.

The archived reconstruction uses an 18-tooth pinion and a sector cut on a 127-tooth pitch at module 0.3 mm. The nominal pitch radii sum to 21.75 mm and the CAD axes are 21.70 mm apart. The preset keeps the 127:18 ratio used by the recorded motion. It uses a common module of 0.29862 mm, giving 0.05 mm extra centre distance, lowers the annular blank to the tooth root and places a pinion gap opposite a sector tooth. This is a geometric clearance allowance. Actual backlash requires measurements of the specimen and the tooth profiles.

The cam sleeve now starts 0.05 mm above the mechanism plate. The opening lug and web occupy Z 12.15–12.55 mm, between the top of the ring at 12.10 and the closing layer at 12.60. The opening stud reaches 12.70 mm. The arresting pin spans 11.55–12.65 mm and joins the closing web. These heights correct the archive's documented clashes within its reconstructed stack (book §13.5).
