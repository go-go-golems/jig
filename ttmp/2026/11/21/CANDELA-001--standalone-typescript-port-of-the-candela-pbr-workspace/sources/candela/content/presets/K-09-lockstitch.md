# Lockstitch head

A household lockstitch sewing machine's mechanism, modelled in **jig** (the CAD workbench) and animated by a **kine** rig: one input, the arm-shaft angle, and a solve block per closed loop — a slider-crank for the needle bar, a four-bar for the take-up lever, eccentrics and a rock shaft for the four-motion feed, a cam for the zigzag gate. The rig was evaluated at 1° steps; `animation(t)` interpolates those frames (lerp of positions, nlerp of rotations) and returns a pose per part.

The data — `lib/lockstitch-data.js` — is generated by `tools/lockstitch/convert.py` from the original page's `meshes.json` (OpenCascade tessellation, a B-rep face per triangle, face kinds) and `rigdata.json`, converted to metres with y up. Each part is a `shape.mesh` per body, grouped under the part's id.

**Finishes from the CAD faces.** Parts carry a material (`steel`, `chrome`, `bronze`, painted cast iron …). The converter finds each lathe part's axis from its largest cylindrical face (the eigenvector of the normals' scatter, then a circle fit) and marks the coaxial cylinders *turned* and the flat faces across the axis *faced*; `.autoFinish()` turns that into anisotropic grooves about the real axis. Links, levers and castings are an isotropic machined finish.

**The scenes** of the original page are the **Scene** choice: each shows its parts and frames its region from the original's view direction (axonometric or side), with an 85 mm lens. In *Whole head* and *Zigzag* the cloth moves with the feed dog while the dog is above the plate, and the seam appears stitch by stitch as the needle goes in (274°).

Not carried over: the section cuts, the drawn thread and the line work of the original — this is the path-traced version.

**Seconds per stitch** sets the playback (one stitch is one turn of the hand wheel; zigzag is two). **Camera orbit** swings the camera about the vertical through the scene's centre, in degrees per stitch — the camera's position is a field of time. Motion blur samples the rig four times across a 180° shutter at 24 fps.

**From the command line**, `tools/lockstitch/render.js` renders the scenes headless through the same render queue — `--scenes 7 --turns 4 --orbit -12` gives four stitches of the whole head with the camera swinging across the front — and `tools/lockstitch/reel.sh` joins them into one captioned MP4.

The clock starts **paused** on a still frame, so the image converges — with its motion blur, which comes from the speed, not from the clock running. Press **Pause** in the viewport to run it.
